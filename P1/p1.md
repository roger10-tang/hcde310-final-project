# P1: Concept Sketches

## Finding someone to golf with at UW

I was thinking about UW students who want to play golf but have trouble finding someone who's free at the same time. My idea is to put tee times from a few golf courses around Seattle on one page and add an “I'm interested” button for each time. If I see a time that works for me, I can tap it, and another student can see that someone wants to play and join me. I'd like the app to pull in available tee times eventually. For a first version, students could share a time they found on a course's booking page, and the app could show the course location and the rain forecast for that time. The main screen would show the course, time, and how many students are interested. You'd pick a day, look through the times, and tap one to show your interest. This fits Multiplayer because everyone sees the same group taking shape. If nobody has shared a time that day, you can add one. While the app loads, it says it's checking the list. If the forecast fails, the tee times stay visible and the forecast is marked unavailable. AI could help compare the booking and weather docs, but we'd need to talk to golfers to figure out what they'd want to know before joining someone.

Data sources are the [GolfCore Course Data API](https://www.golfcore.org/developers/docs/) for names and locations, and the [Open-Meteo Forecast API](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs) for hourly rain chances. Access to a live tee time feed still needs to be confirmed.

## Finding a study buddy at a UW library

My second idea is for UW students who focus better when someone else is sitting there working too. If I'm planning to study at Odegaard from 6 to 8, I can post that time and see if anyone wants to come. We can each work on our own stuff and still have a reason to show up and stay focused. You'd pick a library and a day, then join an invite that fits or post your own. When you post, the app checks that the library will be open for the whole time. The main screen would show an invite with the library, the time, and a button to join. Once someone joins, both students can see the same plan and where to meet, which is the Multiplayer part. If there are no invites at that library, you can start one. While the list is loading, the app says it's checking for invites. If it can't check the library hours, it keeps your unfinished post and lets you try again. AI could help us read the hours data, but we'd need to ask students what would make them comfortable joining a study session with someone new.

Data sources are the [UW Libraries hours table](https://cal.lib.uw.edu/hours/) for opening and closing times, and the [UW campus buildings API](https://gis.maps.uw.edu/federated/rest/services/BaseComponents/SeattleAccessibility/MapServer/25) for library names and addresses.

## Taking the bus to a park with other UW students

A third idea is for UW students who don't have a car and want to go somewhere on the weekend. Say I have Saturday afternoon free and want to get outside, but I'm not sure which parks are easy to reach from campus. I'd choose when I want to leave, and the app would show parks I can get to on one bus from U District. I could see where to board, when the bus leaves, and whether any other students are planning to go. The main screen could show a trip to Magnuson Park with the bus stop and a button to join the group. After picking a park, I could start an outing or join one and meet the group at the stop. The depth item is Many Lenses because the app combines where the parks are with when the buses run to give me options for the time I actually have. If nothing comes up, I can try another departure time. While it loads, the app says it's checking the schedule. If it can't get the bus data, it explains that the times are unavailable and offers a retry. AI could help explain how the transit files fit together, but we'd have to try the trip ourselves to see if the meetup and walk from the stop make sense.

Data sources are the [Seattle Parks dataset](https://data.seattle.gov/d/v5tj-kqhc) for park names and locations, and [King County Metro's GTFS feed](https://kingcounty.gov/en/dept/metro/rider-tools/mobile-and-web-apps) for bus stops, routes, and scheduled departures.
