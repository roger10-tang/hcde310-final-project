# P1: Concept Sketches

## Finding someone to golf with at UW

I started with a problem UW golfers run into when they want to play and can't find anyone who's free. I'd like to put tee times from a few Seattle golf courses in one place. If I see a time I'd play, I tap “I'm interested.” The next person who looks at that time can see that someone else wants to play and decide to join. That's the Multiplayer part. The screen I'd sketch is the list after I've picked a date, with the course, tee time, and number of interested students on each card. For the first version, students can share a slot they found on the course's booking page and see the course location and hourly rain forecast next to it. If we get access to a live tee time feed, the app could pull those times in directly. If nobody has posted for that day, there's a place to add a time. The page shows it's loading while it checks for posts. If the forecast doesn't come through, I'd leave the times on screen and mark the weather as unavailable. AI could help me compare the booking and weather docs, and I'd still need to ask golfers what information they'd want before joining a round.

Data sources are the [GolfCore Course Data API](https://www.golfcore.org/developers/docs/) for names and locations, and the [Open-Meteo Forecast API](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs) for hourly rain chances. Access to a live tee time feed still needs to be confirmed.

## Finding a study buddy at a UW library

For the library idea, I was thinking about how it can be easier to focus when someone else is at the table working too. If I plan to study at Odegaard from 6 to 8, I'd post that time and see if another UW student wants to sit together. We'd each work on our own assignments and have a reason to show up. A student starts by picking a library and a day. The list shows that it’s looking for invites. If it comes back empty, the student can start one. When they post, the app checks that the library is open for the whole session. If the hours can't be checked, they can retry without retyping the invite. I'd sketch the moment someone finds a time that works and joins. Both students can then see the same time and meeting spot, so this one also fits Multiplayer. AI could help me figure out how to read the hours table, while talking to other students would tell me what makes an invite feel comfortable to join.

Data sources are the [UW Libraries hours table](https://cal.lib.uw.edu/hours/) for opening and closing times, and the [UW campus buildings API](https://gis.maps.uw.edu/federated/rest/services/BaseComponents/SeattleAccessibility/MapServer/25) for library names and addresses.

## Taking the bus to a park with other UW students

The third idea is for UW students who want to get out on the weekend and don't have a car. Say I have Saturday afternoon free. I know when I can leave, so I'd enter that time and see which parks I can get to on one bus from U District. That's where Many Lenses comes in. Matching parks to bus stops and scheduled trips gives me a short list of places I can actually get to that afternoon. The screen I'd draw shows a trip to Magnuson Park, where to board, when the bus leaves, and whether anyone is planning to go. I can start an outing or join one and meet the group at the stop. A search would show that it's checking the schedule. If there are no matches, I can try leaving earlier or later. If the times are unavailable because the schedule didn't load, the page would say so and let me retry. AI could help me understand how the transit files fit together, and I'd want to take the trip myself to see how well the meetup and walk from the stop work.

Data sources are the [Seattle Parks dataset](https://data.seattle.gov/d/v5tj-kqhc) for park names and locations, and [King County Metro's GTFS feed](https://kingcounty.gov/en/dept/metro/rider-tools/mobile-and-web-apps) for bus stops, routes, and scheduled departures.
